# Principles: Iman Gadzhi

28 principles, grouped by the job they do: brand and attention, content, focus and self, business model, money, clients. Each has his words and the source id. Quotes from older videos come from auto captions with no punctuation, so they read run-on; a few keep caption errors ("build" for "bill", "pain" for "paying").

## Brand and attention

### 1. You already have a personal brand; the only question is whether it helps or hurts you
Everyone has a digital footprint, and it decides colleges, jobs, clubs, deals and even dating. Doing nothing is a choice with a cost.
- "A personal brand is just the world's perception of you." (S001)
- "we all have a personal brand it's just are you going to use your personal brand to benefit you or is it going to hinder you" (S002)

### 2. Intention first: decide what you sell and who you want, then build the brand backwards
He builds a brand to get rich, not famous. First the product, then the perfect follower, then content that attracts that follower.
- "We knew we wanted to make more money and we found something to sell to make that money." (S001)
- "The goal was never to use our brands to get famous. We want to use our brands to get rich." (S001)

### 3. Audience quality beats audience size
A thousand of the right followers beat millions of the wrong ones. Measure profit per follower, not followers. He runs a 13,000-subscriber business channel next to a 6M one because it speaks only to buyers.
- "You do not need millions of followers to get rich with a personal brand. You just need a thousand of the right ones." (S001)
- "You should be looking at profit per follower." (S001)
- "views do not pay the bills whereas customers do" (S002)

### 4. The brand serves you, never the reverse
No chasing views for their own sake, no being a "dancing monkey". If attention does not move the business, it is noise.
- "you should not be a slave to your personal brand. Your personal brand should serve you in whatever way and through whatever benefits you wish to yield from it." (S001)
- "That doesn't mean that you need to be a dancing monkey, okay?" (S013)

### 5. Be unapologetically yourself and show the journey, not the trophy
Authenticity builds trust even with people who disagree. People connect with the climb and the struggle, not with a finished hero. He has shared voice notes from his darkest months.
- "An audience does not resonate with a hero, they resonate with the journey." (S001)
- "I will never be everyone's cup of tea and that is totally fine, okay?" (S001)

### 6. The human is the moat in the AI era
A personal brand is the one asset nobody can copy. When an audience learns a creator is AI, the creator loses its appeal.
- "it is the one asset that cannot be copied replicated or taken away from you" (S002)
- "the more human you can be in today's day and age the more powerful" (S002)

### 7. Polarization is a signal, not a problem
Backlash from people outside your target means the message is landing. He treats critics as confirmation.
- "it's really just been a green light for me to keep pressing" (S051)
- "I always polarize other agency owners" (S049)

## Content

### 8. Content is distribution, and distribution is the business
A product without people to sell to is worthless. Organic content gets paid for by the platform while it sells for you: negative cost of acquisition.
- "Content matters because it gives you distribution." (S013)
- "Content allows you to achieve something called negative cost of acquisition." (S001)

### 9. You do not need to be an expert, only one step ahead
Level one wants to learn from level five, not level one hundred. Proximity is the advantage of the beginner.
- "you do not need to be an expert you simply need to be one step ahead of someone else" (S002)
- "Level one wants to learn from level five to on how to get to level three." (S001)

### 10. Packaging decides whether good content gets seen
Ideas come from outliers in other niches, repackaged as niche, problem, solution. The hook is the most important line.
- "Most good content is packaged like this: niche, problem, solution, okay?" (S001)
- "A hook is the first line of a video or a piece of copy. It is arguably the most important element of your content." (S001)

### 11. Study content like a student, not a consumer
Scrolling with intention (why did this perform, what was the hook) is one of the most profitable things he does.
- "Actually study what goes into good content and what grabs people's attention." (S013)

### 12. Confidence comes from reps, not from waiting
His first videos were bad and he leaves them public. Volume of reps builds competence, competence builds confidence.
- "confidence comes from competence" (S001)
- "I have posted 439 videos over the last 9 years." (S001)

## Focus and self

### 13. Results follow self-image
The root of every result is identity: what you think you deserve drives your actions, and your actions drive your results.
- "your success is directly related to your self-image, your sense of self-worth." (S012)

### 14. A business problem is usually a personal problem
Sleep, health, focus, social circle and self-image come before tactics. Most people press the gas with their foot on the brake.

### 15. What you work on beats how hard you work
He refuses the "just work harder" message. Picking the right vehicle is most of the game.
- "The simple truth is that it is not about how hard you work. It's about what you work on." (S021)

### 16. Focus is the scarce resource; protect it like money
Delete distractions, cut social media you don't use for business, keep a boring routine, no networking for its own sake, no travel while building.
- "your relationships your business traveling is a terrible idea" (S055)
- "you eliminate as many distractions as possible" (S043)

### 17. Results first, then people come to you
Instead of networking up, get results and meet people as equals.

### 18. Say no to almost everything
Success came from turning down deals that were 90% fits. Early on, take 60% fits; later, protect the machine. He calls it "my cup runneth over".

### 19. Delayed gratification and sacrifice set the size of the result
The slingshot: the further you pull back (sacrifice, private victories), the further you fly. Decide how big you want your life, then pay that price.
- "people are very bad at delayed gratification." (S006)
- "you can't try to cash in your public victory too quick." (S006)

## Business model

### 20. One niche, one service: narrow and deep
Specialists get paid more, deliver easier and compound referrals. Two niches is two businesses.

### 21. Traction, not addition
Adding services is the tempting way to grow and the wrong one. Do more of the thing that already works.

### 22. The problem you solve must be simple, even if the method is complex
Anyone should understand what you fix. The complexity lives in the how.
- "the problem that you solve shouldn't be complex the problem that you solve anyone should understand" (S053)

### 23. Watch the customer, not the competition
Every unit of attention spent on rivals is taken from the customer. He cites Bezos and Buffett.
- "when you focus on the competition you really take the eye off the ball" (S050)
- "business is all about creating value for people" (S052)

### 24. You are the main asset; invest in yourself first
At the start you have no leverage, no team, no audience. Skills compound faster than money.
- "You are the number one biggest income-producing asset that you have." (S011)
- "it's these high income skills that you need to stack up" (S060)

## Money

### 25. How you make money matters more than how much
Freedom comes from the vehicle, not the number. A business that needs you 24/7 owns you.
- "it's much more about how you make your money then how much money you make" (S023)
- "well then you don't own the business, owns you." (S014)

### 26. Respect money and stay emotionally indifferent to it
Know the difference between price and cost, negotiate, treat opportunity cost as the biggest expense, and never let swings move your heart rate.
- "money is not moral. It's infrastructure." (S007)
- "the moment money excites you or scares you, you've lost." (S007)
- "the biggest expense in your life will be opportunity cost." (S019)

## Clients

### 27. Every recall is a reframe: remove friction from the experience
Each time a client has to think about paying you (a manual invoice, a chase, a mistake), they re-judge the whole relationship, whatever the results. Auto-bill, keep one channel, and avoid small errors, because they pile up into the decision to leave.

### 28. You attract what you are
Good clients come to people who are good clients themselves: thankful, respectful, paying on time. Bad customers tend to have bad customers.
