# Positions: Iman Gadzhi

What he thinks on concrete topics, with his words and source. Where his view changed over time, both versions are given with dates. Agency-era views (2017 to 2020) come from a period when he ran IAG Media; current views (2024 to 2026) come from his education, investing and AI-product era.

---

## Personal brand
The most valuable asset a person can own, and the one he credits for most of his wealth. Built with intention for a sale, not for fame; small and qualified beats big; face beats faceless.
- "hand on heart, if I had to attribute one thing towards my success over the last [music] 10 years, it is my ability to build an intentional personal brand." (S001)
- "a personal brand where you show your face will always be better than a personal brand where you don't." (S001)

## Organic content vs paid ads
Organic content is the holy grail because it gets paid to acquire customers (one VSL earned $266k in AdSense and drove about $20M in sales). He still runs paid retargeting and used paid ads to fill his agency; he is not against ads, he is against paying for what content can do for free.
- "YouTube paid me $250,000 to make 20 million dollars." (S001)

## Short form vs long form
Long form, especially YouTube, is where buyers spend hours with you; short form widens reach. He splits audiences by platform rather than forcing one format on everyone, and does not chase shorts hacks.
- "I'm not going to give you some advice there some uh you YouTube shorts hacks that's going to get you millions of views but what what are those views worth" (S002)
- "when someone watches your YouTube videos they spend hours and hours with you" (S002)

## Virality and luck
Virality has luck in it; volume is how you remove the luck. Same logic for products: unlimited pulls at the slot machine.
- "the only way to actually mitigate luck is to post UGC at scale with a lot of volume across different platforms." (S058)
- "Imagine if you had unlimited pulls at a slot machine. Eventually, you'd win." (S011)

## Creators and monetization
Most creators have attention and trust but no product; brand deals are unpredictable. The money is in digital products sold to an audience that already trusts you. He bought into Whop and Consulting.com on this thesis.
- "the biggest creators in the world aren't making their money from brand deals and affiliate links. They're making their money from digital products" (S014)

## AI
Positive and opportunistic: the window is open now, tools lower the barrier, and AI makes the human brand more valuable, not less. Since 2025 most of his public content sells AI-based business models and his own AI tools (Higgsfield investment, Consulting.com software), so treat his AI enthusiasm as partly commercial.
- "Every major opportunity feels obvious when it's gone." (S016)
- "will never be able to remove the humanness out of you" (S002)

## Agencies (SMMA)
For years he said an agency is the best business model to reach $10k to $50k a month profit: low capital, high margin, contractors deliver. Rules: one service, one niche, no free trials, never handle client ad spend, bill on day one, three-month minimum, the client pays the platform directly. His current flagship content reframes the agency as helping micro-creators monetize.
- "that's not what I believe is the best business model to make 10 20 30 40 50 thousand dollars a month profit for me it's agency" (S060)
- "you never ever ever want to charge add spin you charge just your service fee" (S044)

## Free trials and guarantees
Against free trials: they attract clients you do not want, they do not convert, and paid clients refer just as well. Guarantees only pro-rated, never full refunds.
- "anyone who's offering free trials quite clearly you wouldn't want to work with them anyways" (S054)

## Copywriting
The core skill behind ads, emails and retention; learned by volume and a swipe file, and best ads come from intuition, not data. He told clients who left that copy was the reason.
- "the biggest reason that you guys are losing clients that you're advertising for is because your copy is not good enough" (S060)

## Ads that look like ads
An ad should look like native content; stock images and slogans lose.
- "if the person looking at it can tell it's an ad you've done" (S044)

## Cold outreach
Targeted Loom audits (10 to 25 minutes) beat mass messages for online businesses; he pitches products he personally uses. Most cold emails fail because nobody does their homework. Present your one service as the best channel there is.

## Client retention
Clients usually leave at month three because results make the work look easy to bring in house. Retention comes from results, copy, and delight; the highest payers tend to be the least demanding.
- "if clients leave pretty much always they're leaving at the three-month mark" (S041)

## Networking
Mostly a waste in the build phase. Get results and people come to you as equals. Networking rooms are full of ego.
- "when you're networking a lot of ego is involved" (S053)

## Business partners
Against them for agency owners: most people want one out of loneliness or to look cool, and unequal contribution breeds resentment.
- "I have never found anyone who can bring more value to the table than me that actually wants a partnership" (S046)

## Travel and variation
In 2018 he called travel and constant novelty a business killer; routine frees focus. Later he spent months in Cape Town, Bali and Dubai, but framed them as focus retreats or earned breaks.
- "your relationships your business traveling is a terrible idea" (S055)

## Remote vs office
Changed. He built his first businesses fully remote and taught remote agencies (2019 to 2020). By 2026 he prefers in-person teams for top-tier companies.
- "I've never seen a truly savage company, like top-tier company, built remotely. And I say that someone who built all my early companies remotely." (S005)

## Social media for personal use
Toxic for focus. In the course he tells students to delete Instagram and says having social media early was his biggest regret; he uses it only to grow the business.

## and gurus
Against hustle porn, sleep-deprivation bragging, motivational quotes and "just work harder". Systems beat affirmations shouted in the morning.
- "I hate this whole thing online of like, you just need to work harder." (S021)

## Sleep and health
Non-negotiable base for focus. Seven to nine hours; anyone proud of four hours is not serious.

## Education system and college
Against the traditional route; his stated mission is to reform education, and he builds schools in Nepal and South Africa. Self-education and skill stacking compound faster than degrees.
- "I don't think you need to go to college to achieve the goals" (S003)
- "you can use skill stacking and double your Revenue every couple of months" (S024)

## Passion
Keep passion and money apart. Choose something practical; keep hobbies as hobbies; his eyewear brand is a passion project he does not run for profit. In niche choice he softens this: interest in the client's business helps you sell and deliver.
- "I've always been very good at keeping my passion away from like how I make my money." (S008)
- "keep your hobbies your hobbies" (S009)

## Competition
Ignore it unless you plan to acquire it. Customer obsession over competitor watching.
- "when you focus on the competition you really take the eye off the ball" (S050)

## Money and happiness
Money does not make you happy, but you only internalize that after getting it. Hedonism is not happiness; never put a goal on a pedestal (buying his mother's house led to their worst three months).
- "hedonism does not equal happiness" (S037)
- "never ever put something on a pedestal" (S028)

## Investing
Make money in the business, multiply it with investments later. Early, invest in yourself and tools. Buy assets when everyone is scared, not when they boom. Net worth matters once you build a company to sell.
- "your business should always be your number one focus" (S043)

## Opinions
Speak your mind, take edgy positions, but stay silent where you have not done the work. Everyone can have an opinion; not everyone deserves a say.
- "never be afraid to speak what's on your mind" (S003)
- "everyone deserves an opinion everyone can have an opinion but not everyone deserves to say it" (S031)

## Packages and bundles
Against them. One service at one price; clients only look at the piece that makes them money anyway.

## Client selection
Take only clients whose profit pays for their craziness; very crazy but very profitable ones get the three-month minimum and then go. Even a client his agency drove 40% to 70% of revenue for left after three months, which he blames on small early mistakes.

## Pricing
Charge by value and by client, not fixed packages; premium pricing follows perceived authority from content. Bonus framing: offer more than expected up front.
- "your pricing should be different per client" (S034)
