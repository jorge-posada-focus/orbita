# Frameworks: Iman Gadzhi

20 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (brand, content, niche and offer, retention and attention, self, clients and outreach). "Sources" lists the ids where he uses the framework or a clear synonym. Score options 1 to 10 unless a framework says otherwise.

---

## Brand

### 1. Intentional personal brand (4 steps)
- **What it is:** A brand built backwards from a sale, not forwards from a hobby. Steps: (1) clarify intention, (2) pick the platform, (3) create content, (4) monetize.
- **Parts:** Intention = what you sell + who your perfect follower is + how to attract more of them. Platform = where that follower already spends time, what suits your skills and hides your weaknesses, face or faceless (face always wins). Content = pillars and types (frameworks 4 and 5). Monetize = products that fit the audience (courses, services, community).
- **When to use:** Any decision about a brand, a channel or a content plan; before anyone posts "to grow".
- **How to score:** Can the person name the product and the ideal buyer in one sentence? If not, score 0 and stop. Then score the platform on match to buyer, match to skill, and face.
- **Quote:** "first thing, ask yourself, what are you going to sell? What do you want to sell? Then, what does your perfect follower or subscriber, whatever it is, look like? And then how do you attract more of these followers?" (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S013, S022, S057

### 2. Micro personal brand and profit per follower
- **What it is:** Optimize for audience quality, not size. A few thousand followers who all want what you sell can fund a business; millions of the wrong ones cannot.
- **Parts:** Ideal follower defined by buying power and need. Siloed channels per segment (main channel for beginners, business channel for owners above $250k a year, vlog channel for a product brand). Content that repels the wrong people on purpose (technical thumbnails on the business channel).
- **When to use:** Choosing who to speak to; judging a post or channel by its numbers.
- **How to score:** Estimate revenue per follower, not reach. A post with fewer views but more qualified leads scores higher.
- **Quote:** "A micro personal brand is a brand that doesn't optimize for audience size, but focuses on audience quality." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S022

### 3. Brand and business flywheel (document everything)
- **What it is:** The personal brand gives the business authority; the business gives the brand proof; each feeds the other. Document the journey so the proof exists later.
- **Parts:** Make content in your field, win clients through it, show the clients as evidence, attract bigger clients, repeat.
- **When to use:** When a founder asks whether content is "worth the time".
- **How to score:** Does each piece of content create proof that sells the business, and does each business win create content? Both yes = flywheel.
- **Quote:** "as my personal brand grows even bigger marketing agency continues to grow" (S057)
- **Sources:** S057, S002, S001

## Content

### 4. Three content pillars: authenticity, impact, intention
- **What it is:** Every piece should be true to you, solve a problem, and attract the right audience.
- **Parts:** Authenticity (be unapologetically you; show the journey). Impact (value through teaching, presenting new opportunities, or sharing experience; you only need to be one step ahead). Intention (attract buyers, not a big audience).
- **When to use:** Reviewing a script, post or calendar.
- **How to score:** 1 to 10 on each pillar. Any pillar under 5 means rewrite.
- **Quote:** "Authenticity is content that is true to you. Impact is content that solves a problem. And intention is content that attracts your perfect audience." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S002

### 5. Superficial vs in-depth content (3:1 growth to nurture)
- **What it is:** Two content types with two jobs. Superficial makes you known (story, trust, wide reach). In-depth makes you money (education, authority). He runs about three growth pieces for every nurture piece.
- **Parts:** Categories: actionable (show how), narrative (tell a story), theoretical (why), attractor (share results). Actionable and theoretical lean in-depth; narrative and attractor lean superficial.
- **When to use:** Planning a weekly calendar; diagnosing "lots of views, no sales" (too superficial) or "sales but no growth" (too in-depth).
- **How to score:** Tag each planned piece; check the mix and that the in-depth pieces point to what you sell.
- **Quote:** "you've got superficial which makes you known and then you have in-depth that makes you money" (S002)
- **Sources:** S001, S002

### 6. Content trifecta: content, plan, packaging
- **What it is:** Content is what you create, plan is how you create it, packaging is how you present it (title, thumbnail, hook). Packaging decides whether the content is seen.
- **Parts:** Ideas from outliers in other niches (VidIQ multiples), repackaged as niche + problem + solution. Long form: power of three (big idea, three points, three subpoints, takeaway). Hooks: bold claim, curiosity, or promise of value. Proven formats such as brand leveraging (use a famous case to teach) and ultimate guides.
- **When to use:** Ideation and scripting; when good content gets no views.
- **How to score:** Does the idea come from a proven outlier? Is the problem clear in the title? Does the first line use one of the three hook types?
- **Quote:** "content is what you create plan is how you create it and packaging is how you present it" (S002)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S013

### 7. Study other niches, not competitors
- **What it is:** Copying your own niche keeps you one step behind. Take what works elsewhere and bring it in.
- **Parts:** Find outliers (videos performing many times above the creator's average) in unrelated niches; keep the structure; insert your niche, problem and solution.
- **When to use:** Every ideation session.
- **How to score:** Ideas taken from a competitor score low; ideas adapted from an outlier in another niche score high.
- **Quote:** "don't waste your time studying your competitors because you will always be one step behind instead study creators from completely different niches" (S002)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S050

## Niche and offer

### 8. Niche selection: three questions plus a six-month hall pass
- **What it is:** Test a niche before committing. Try anything for six months, then lock into one.
- **Parts:** (1) Does the client make at least $5,000 a month (can they afford you)? (2) Are you passionate, or at least interested and intrigued? (3) Do you understand the mechanics of their business (speak their language)? Then research: business model, benchmarks, niche-specific tactics and case studies.
- **When to use:** Choosing a market, an ICP, or a sector for content.
- **How to score:** Three gates; failing question 1 kills the niche. Then score 2 and 3.
- **Sources:** S061, S069, S049, S042

### 9. Lean, mean, results-driven agency (one service, one niche)
- **What it is:** His agency philosophy: one results-driven service for one niche, delivered by a predetermined machine, lean team, resourceful.
- **Parts:** Single KPI the client understands (leads, conversion value). Machine (the client's sales process) must work before you feed it traffic. Contractor arbitrage for delivery. Communication only through one channel. Achieve more with less.
- **When to use:** Designing any service business or deciding whether to add a service.
- **How to score:** Count services and niches (more than one each = score low). Check whether a new client slots into the same system unchanged.
- **Quote:** "You need to achieve more with less resources." (S042)
- **Sources:** S042, S049, S061, S067, S044, S081

### 10. Offer = niche + service + pricing model, then packaging
- **What it is:** Two agencies with the same niche, service and price can have different offers because of how they package them.
- **Parts:** Niche, service, pricing model (retainer, retainer plus percentage of return), packaging. No free trials. Guarantees only as a pro-rated refund, never full money back.
- **When to use:** Building or repricing an offer.
- **How to score:** Are all three variables defined? Is the packaging distinct from competitors at the same price?
- **Quote:** "an offer is the following your Niche plus your service plus your pricing model those three variables and then how you package it" (S022)
- **Sources:** S022, S054, S044

### 11. Business model screen: four metrics
- **What it is:** How he judges any business model for a beginner: startup capital, profit margin, demand, fulfillment. Later versions add speed to first dollar, skill needed, scalability and how much attention the model demands.
- **Parts:** Best beginner models have low capital, high margin, high demand, easy fulfillment. A model that needs constant attention owns you.
- **When to use:** Comparing new business lines or side projects.
- **How to score:** Score each metric; weight capital and attention heavily for a founder with no cash or team.
- **Quote:** "you always need to look at these four metrics startup Capital profit margin demand and fulfillment" (S022)
- **Sources:** S022, S010, S014, S023

## Retention and attention

### 12. Staying top of mind: retargeting plus a story-based weekly email
- **What it is:** Everyone who touches your site sees you for months; everyone on your list gets a weekly story email. Cheap, and proof you use your own medicine.
- **Parts:** Retargeting audience of site visitors (a few dollars a day). Weekly emails written as a Sunday ritual: a story or insight, with the call to action tied into the story. Never a hard-sell newsletter.
- **When to use:** Any lead that did not buy yet; nurturing an audience between launches.
- **How to score:** Is there a retargeting pool? Is there a weekly value email? Is the CTA inside the story?
- **Sources:** S065, S002

### 13. Three barometers for social media work: growth, convenience, brand equity
- **What it is:** Social media management rarely shows a clean ROI, so agree up front which of three outcomes the client is buying.
- **Parts:** Social growth (followers, page likes). Convenience (one area of the business fully off their hands, cheaper than an employee). Brand equity (the profile looks as big as the business; congruence).
- **When to use:** Selling or reporting content and social media services.
- **How to score:** Is one barometer named in writing before work starts? If not, expect conflict.
- **Sources:** S064

### 14. Framing: turn a loss into a gain for the other side
- **What it is:** Price rises and new terms are presented so the client feels they win. A higher retainer feels like a loss; a small percentage of return "aligns incentives".
- **Parts:** Name what they got for free so far, propose the change as smaller than what is fair, show how it benefits them. For referrals, offer the partner more up front than they expect (50% to 60% of month one) and ask the same back.
- **When to use:** Renegotiations, upsells, partnerships.
- **How to score:** Would the other side describe the change as good for them? If not, reframe.
- **Sources:** S067, S066

## Self

### 15. Slingshot effect (sacrifice sets the ceiling)
- **What it is:** Success is proportional to how far you pull back: months of sacrifice and private victories before any public one. Decide the size of the life you want, then accept the sacrifice that size requires.
- **Parts:** Micro view (the next 14 days of choices), macro view (the next 3 years). Private victories before public victory. Re-decide how much you are willing to give as you grow.
- **When to use:** Setting goals, judging whether a plan matches the ambition.
- **How to score:** Compare the stated goal with the sacrifices actually planned. A 10/10 goal with 5/10 sacrifice fails.
- **Quote:** "you need to decide how extraordinary you want your physical slash material world to be because that's decides how extraordinary your dedication sacrifice is" (S045)
- **Sources:** S045, S006, S025, S031

### 16. Monk mode and the six pillars of the operator
- **What it is:** Periods of extreme focus built on a fixed protocol, plus the base that makes focus possible.
- **Parts:** Six pillars from his course: focus, health and sleep, social circle, sense of self, visualization and affirmations, momentum. Monk mode: three non-negotiables (he uses meditation, strict diet, no alcohol or weed) plus a few chosen variables, run in cycles with short breaks.
- **When to use:** When a founder is distracted, inconsistent, or "working hard" without results.
- **How to score:** Check sleep, distractions removed, routine fixed, social circle supportive. Fix the base before adding tactics.
- **Quote:** "you stack other variables and you get to choose the other variables" (S036)
- **Sources:** S062, S036, S025, S070, S043

## Clients and outreach

### 17. Opportunity offer vs convenience offer (and no packages)
- **What it is:** A convenience offer is work the client could do or learn themselves but would rather pay to skip; an opportunity offer gives them something they could not get alone. Opportunity offers get paid more. Sell one service, never bundled packages.
- **Parts:** Classify the service (accountant = convenience, tax advisor = opportunity). Lead with the part that brings the most revenue or saves the most time. One service at the same price a package would fetch; no silver and gold tiers.
- **When to use:** Choosing or pitching a service, setting price, deciding whether to bundle.
- **How to score:** Could the client do this themselves in a week? If yes, it is convenience: price it lower or find the opportunity angle. Count services in the offer; more than one scores low.
- **Sources:** S081

### 18. Craziness vs profitability: which clients to take
- **What it is:** Score every client on two axes, how crazy (demanding, difficult) and how profitable. Every client is a little crazy; the question is whether the money pays for it.
- **Parts:** No-go zone (low profit, mid to high craziness): decline. Three-month-minimum zone (very profitable but very crazy): take it, deliver the minimum term, then cut it off. Bliss zone (moderate craziness, high profit): keep and grow. Three rules once signed: boundaries (perceived value is set by the limits you hold), friendship (become friends early, send a small gift when they sign), reflection (be a good client to others).
- **When to use:** Qualifying a prospect on the call, deciding whether to renew, explaining why a client went bad.
- **How to score:** Craziness 4 to 10, profitability 0 to 10. Profit at 5 or below with craziness 4 or above = no.
- **Sources:** S086, S085, S087

### 19. Sell the meeting, not the service; then let data pick the channel
- **What it is:** Outreach has one job: get the meeting. The service, the price and the pitch belong to the call. Test channels (cold call, email, Loom, DMs, walk-ins), track them, then double down on the one that brings the best clients and refine it.
- **Parts:** Sell the idea of a meeting. Make it feel low commitment (short, specific time). Only upside for them, whatever happens. Track attempts, meetings, closes and retainer value per channel; the winner is the one with the best clients, not only the most meetings.
- **When to use:** Writing a first message, a cold call opener, or deciding where to spend outreach time.
- **How to score:** Does the first message mention the service or the price? If yes, rewrite it. Is there data per channel before choosing?
- **Sources:** S088, S091, S063, S089

### 20. Raise the baseline through identity, and visualize the process
- **What it is:** Willpower spikes break; lasting output comes from slowly raising your baseline (what is normal and acceptable for you) by changing identity. Visualization should rehearse the sticking points of the work, not the Ferrari.
- **Parts:** Name your non-negotiables (his: wake at 6:30 six days a week, no phone until lunch, a monthly revenue floor). Grow capacity slowly so the pace speeds up over time instead of burning out. Pick one skill or sticking point and visualize pushing through it daily. Be gentle with yourself as a beginner; get harder only once identity holds.
- **When to use:** A founder who starts strong and quits, or who relies on motivation.
- **How to score:** Are the non-negotiables written and kept for weeks? Does the visualization target a process step?
- **Sources:** S084, S083, S096, S062
